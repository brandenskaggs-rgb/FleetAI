The founder story

Branden Skaggs worked in construction and spent years traveling between job sites. He repeatedly saw work trucks broken down on highways and roadsides, and watched reactive maintenance become the norm across the industry. Even companies he worked for relied on unreliable vehicles, delays, and lost time. Breakdowns were expected, not prevented.

After that early field experience, Branden founded Skaggs Fleet Service LLC, a mobile mechanic and fleet service business. Through this work he repaired cars, trucks, and fleet vehicles firsthand, diagnosing failures and working through maintenance schedules, downtime, and cost exposure. It was direct mechanical and operational experience, not theory.

Repeated exposure to breakdowns raised a practical question: why are inspections and repairs so often reactive? Why is data collected but rarely used predictively? As AI and machine learning became more accessible, he started connecting those operational questions with emerging analytical capabilities.

The idea came from the field, but the software was built at home. Development happened outside of work hours through nights, weekends, and long sessions of focused work. Branden taught himself software development, system architecture, data handling, and frontend and backend fundamentals. Progress required iteration, failure, rebuilding, and refinement.

Many people did not initially understand the vision, and some doubted its feasibility. Progress was slow at times and required consistency. The work continued regardless.

Today, Fleet AI exists because of firsthand fleet experience combined with self-taught engineering. The platform is designed to reduce downtime, anticipate failures, and improve operational clarity with a focus on uptime, predictability, compliance, cost awareness, and actionable intelligence.

Operational principles
  • Respect the realities of fleet uptime and maintenance scheduling
  • Keep intelligence fleet-specific with data separation
  • Build tools for operators, not dashboards for vanity

What we believe

Fleet AI is built for operators, drivers, and decision makers. It is not built for hype, trends, or vanity metrics. It exists to make fleet operations more predictable and resilient.

Predictive maintenance, not reactive alerts

Risk-based guidance should arrive before downtime, not after a breakdown.

Fleet intelligence, not data noise

Executive-ready insights should be clear enough to act on the same day.

Respect the operator workflow

The platform must fit how fleets actually run, not how software teams wish they did.
